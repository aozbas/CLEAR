# CLEAR Project Governance

## Official project

Alpaslan Ozbas founded CLEAR and is the lead maintainer of the official project. The lead maintainer
has final responsibility for the official repository, roadmap, accepted scope, merge decisions,
releases, security response, public claims, and use of official project branding.

Forking the source code does not grant administrative rights in the official repository or the
right to present a modified distribution as an official CLEAR release.

## Contributions and ownership

The founder retains copyright in their original contributions. Each contributor retains copyright
in their own contributions unless a separate written agreement says otherwise. Accepted
contributions are licensed under the applicable repository license and certified through the
[Developer Certificate of Origin](DCO); they are not assigned to the founder merely by being
accepted.

The project name, concept, roadmap, source-code copyright, contributor copyrights, and repository
administration are distinct. The [Mozilla Public License 2.0](LICENSE) grants rights to covered
source code; it does not transfer copyright ownership or grant rights to project marks.

## Decisions

Maintainers evaluate proposals based on:

- fit with the educational, technical, privacy-first demo scope;
- software correctness, readability, testability, and maintainability;
- privacy, security, and sensitive-data risk;
- accurate experimental and non-diagnostic framing;
- reproducibility and honest evidence boundaries; and
- compatibility with third-party licenses and data-use terms.

Discussion and reasoned disagreement are welcome. The lead maintainer makes the final decision when
consensus is not reached and may reject, defer, revise, or revert changes that do not meet the
official project's requirements.

## Maintainer changes

The lead maintainer may appoint additional maintainers and delegate areas of responsibility.
Material governance changes should be documented in this file. Licensing existing contributions
under materially different terms requires the rights and permissions applicable to those
contributions; repository administration alone does not erase contributor copyrights.
